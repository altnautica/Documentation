# Hardware

Vision Navigation needs a **downward-facing camera**. The `optical_flow` mode also needs a **rangefinder** that reports distance to the ground; `optical_flow_degraded` runs without one by taking height from the flight controller's barometer.

The companion board must be one the manifest lists: Raspberry Pi 5, CM5, CM4, Zero 2 W, Radxa ROCK 5C Lite (RK3582), Radxa CM4 (RK3588S2), or an RK3576 board. See [Supported boards](/drone-agent/vision-nav-overview#supported-boards).

## Camera

The agent's vision host owns the camera and publishes frames on the shared vision bus; the plugin reads them from there. The plugin accepts `nv12`, `yuv420p`, and `rgb24` frames and works in grayscale. 640x480 at 30 fps is plenty for the tracker, and the flow rate follows the camera frame rate. Rolling-shutter cameras are fine; a global shutter handles fast motion better.

### USB UVC

Plug-and-play. The vision host enumerates `/dev/video*` and captures the camera. Good picks:

- USB inspection or borescope cameras with a fixed-focus lens around 60 to 90 degrees field of view. Cheap and mechanically simple.
- USB UVC camera modules with an M12 lens mount, so you can choose the field of view.
- Webcams with manual focus. Avoid autofocus-only models; the tracker loses features while the lens hunts.

### MIPI CSI

Lower latency, a little more setup, and worth it on boards with a CSI port (Raspberry Pi, Radxa, RK3576 boards). Good picks:

- Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3, wide variant (120 degrees), suits a downward mount.
- IMX219 or IMX477 modules, on Raspberry Pi or Radxa with the matching adapter cable.
- Match the FFC cable pitch to the board (0.5 mm versus 1.0 mm). The wrong pitch can destroy the connector.

Set `camera.bus_type` to `uvc` or `csi` and `camera.orientation` to `downward` in the plugin's configuration.

### Lens field of view

Aim for **60 to 100 degrees diagonal**. A narrow lens sees too little ground at low altitude to keep enough corners in view; an ultra-wide lens adds distortion. The wide variant of a common module is usually a better start than the default narrow one.

## Rangefinder

The tracker on its own measures angular velocity (radians per second). Height turns that into linear velocity (metres per second), which is what the EKF wants.

### Flight controller relayed (recommended)

If the rangefinder is wired to the flight controller, the flight controller streams `DISTANCE_SENSOR` and the plugin reads it. No extra wiring, and it works with any sensor the flight controller supports. Set `rangefinder.topology` to `fc`. On iNav, set `rangefinder_hardware` for the wired sensor.

The trade-off is a little latency, since the reading travels flight controller to MAVLink to plugin. Readings older than 200 ms count as missing.

### Companion owned

The plugin can read a rangefinder on the companion directly. Set `rangefinder.topology` to `companion` and pick the driver.

| Driver | Sensor | Bus | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tfluna_uart` | Benewake TF-Luna | UART | Implemented. 0.2 to 8 m, low cost. The plugin also forwards its readings to the flight controller as `DISTANCE_SENSOR`. |
| `garmin_lidarlite_i2c` | Garmin LIDAR-Lite v3 | I2C | Not available yet: returns no reading until the plugin SDK exposes I2C. Wire it to the flight controller and use `fc`. 0.05 to 40 m. |
| `vl53l1x_i2c` | ST VL53L1X | I2C | Not available yet, same reason. Wire it to the flight controller. 0.04 to 4 m. |

The plugin requests `hardware.uart` and `hardware.i2c`; you grant them at install time. Wire the TF-Luna's power (per its datasheet), ground, and TX/RX to a free UART on the companion, then set `rangefinder.device` (for example `/dev/ttyAMA0`) and `rangefinder.baud` (115200 by default).

## Mounting

The camera goes on the bottom of the airframe, lens straight down, with a clear view of the ground.

- **Keep landing gear out of frame.** Even thin gear legs make strong features that don't move with the ground.
- **Keep props out of frame.** A recessed mount or a guard usually fixes it.
- **Mount rigidly.** Soft mounts add camera lag relative to the gyro, which hurts derotation.
- **Point the rangefinder the same way.** Straight down, close to the camera. Offset matters near the ground.

## Ground clearance

The rangefinder has to read inside its valid range. Outside the band the flight controller is configured for, flow fusion stops.

- TF-Luna: fly at 0.3 m or higher; the bottom 0.2 m is unreliable.
- ST VL53L1X: fine from 0.1 m; treat 3 m as the practical ceiling.
- Garmin LIDAR-Lite v3: fine from 0.1 m, with the most headroom of the three.

The pre-arm card checks that a rangefinder is configured, not that it is reading. Watch the distance on the Vision Nav sensors card before you arm.

## Wiring example

A typical layout on a Radxa ROCK 5C Lite:

```text
USB-A or CSI port        -> downward camera
UART TX/RX (pins 8/10)   -> TF-Luna (optional, companion-owned)
GND                      -> shared ground with the flight controller
FC MAVLink UART          -> companion (already wired for the agent)
```

On a Raspberry Pi 5 it looks the same: CSI port for a Pi camera module, a UART on pins 8/10 for a TF-Luna. I2C rangefinders go on the flight controller, not the companion.

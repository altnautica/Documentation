A single ground station hears the drone from one antenna at one place. That is enough most of the time. It is not enough when a hill is in the way, when the flight area is long and narrow, or when you want a second receiver in case the first loses line of sight.

Distributed receive puts two or three ground stations around the flight area, links them over a small private mesh, and has them share the receive job. The drone transmits once. The mesh combines what each node heard into one clean stream.

## When to deploy a mesh

A mesh helps when:

- Terrain, buildings or vegetation block line of sight from a single ground point.
- You want a redundant receiver.
- You need coverage along a long corridor (a road, a fence line, a runway).
- Only one node has an internet uplink but every node should reach the cloud.

You do not need one when a single ground station at the takeoff point sees the whole flight.

## The shape

| Role | What it does |
|---|---|
| **direct** | Single-node receive. No mesh. The default. |
| **relay** | Forwards what it hears from the drone to a receiver over the mesh. |
| **receiver** | The hub. Combines its own reception with every paired relay's forwards and serves the stream to clients. |

A mesh has exactly one receiver and one or more relays. A `direct` node never takes part in a mesh. [Pick a role](/ground-agent/setup#pick-a-role) compares the three.

## How the nodes find each other

Each relay and the receiver carry a **second RTL8812EU or RTL8812AU adapter** for the mesh; the first stays on the drone link. The mesh adapter runs batman-adv, a kernel mesh that handles routing, neighbour discovery and self-healing. The receiver advertises `_ados-receiver._tcp` over mDNS on `bat0`, and relays forward to the receiver that answers.

Node-to-node range depends on the adapters, antennas, terrain and channel. Plan spacing from a site survey with your own hardware.

See [Local mesh (batman-adv)](/ground-agent/batman-adv).

## How nodes pair

Pairing is local. No cloud service is involved.

- The receiver opens a 60-second accept window from Mission Control (**Mesh & RX**) or the REST API and shows a six-digit join code.
- Each relay joins with that code.
- The receiver approves the relay, and an encrypted invite (mesh id, shared key, drone receive key) goes back over the mesh. It opens only under the join code, so a node that overheard the request cannot answer it.
- The relay stores the invite, then changes its role to `relay`.

See [Build a mesh](/ground-agent/mesh-setup#mesh-pairing).

## What you do not manage

- **Routing.** batman-adv handles hops, reroutes, new neighbours and dead-neighbour eviction.
- **Cloud uplink.** If any node has Ethernet, a Wi-Fi client link or a cellular modem, the mesh can use it as the cloud gateway, and fails over to another when it drops.
- **FEC combine.** The Reed-Solomon recovery runs at the receiver over fragments from every radio.
- **Serving the stream.** Browsers, phones and the HDMI kiosk connect to the receiver exactly as they would to a single node.

## What you do manage

- **Hardware.** Every relay and the receiver need the second WFB-class adapter.
- **Channels.** The drone link runs on 5 GHz; the mesh runs on 2.4 GHz (channel 1 by default, `ground_station.mesh.channel`), so the two radios do not interfere. Pick the mesh channel for the site's 2.4 GHz conditions.
- **Roles.** Decide which node is the receiver before you go out. It is also where Mission Control and the pilot connect.
